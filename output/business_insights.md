# Business Insights — TaskFlow SaaS Metrics

Source: `output/mrr_by_month.csv`, `mrr_waterfall.csv`, `churn_rate_by_month.csv`,
`nrr_by_month.csv`, `ltv_cac_by_channel.csv`, `cohort_retention.csv` —
generated by `scripts/02_build_monthly_snapshot.py` and
`scripts/03_calculate_metrics.py` from the Jan 2023–Dec 2024 event ledger.

**Assumption used throughout:** gross margin = 80% (not present in the raw
data — used for every LTV calculation below).

---

## Q1 — Is TaskFlow growing in a healthy way, or leaking revenue?

**Finding.** Total MRR grew from **$1,327 (Jan 2023) to $93,204 (Dec 2024)**
— a ~70x increase. But the MRR waterfall shows that growth is almost
entirely powered by new customers, not the existing base:

| 24-month total | Amount |
|---|---|
| New MRR (new signups) | +$102,390 |
| Expansion MRR (upgrades) | +$18,965 |
| Contraction MRR (downgrades) | −$4,399 |
| Churned MRR (cancellations) | −$23,752 |
| **Net change from existing customers** | **−$9,186** |

Net Revenue Retention averages **98.7%** across the period (98.9% over the
last 6 months) — consistently just under 100%. Every dollar of MRR sitting
in the existing customer base is, on net, shrinking slightly month over
month; 100% of net growth comes from acquisition.

**So what.** Growth is real but structurally fragile: it depends on the
acquisition engine running at full speed indefinitely. If new-customer
volume slows for any reason (budget cuts, channel saturation, market
conditions), MRR growth stalls immediately because the existing base is not
self-sustaining on its own.

**Recommendation.** Treat NRR, not just headline MRR, as the primary growth
health metric on the exec dashboard. Set a target of NRR ≥ 100% (ideally
105–110%, typical for healthy B2B SaaS) and instrument the two levers that
move it — expansion (upsell Free→Pro→Business motion) and revenue churn —
separately, since they currently roughly cancel each other out.

## Q2 — Which acquisition channel brings the best-quality customers (highest LTV:CAC)?

**Finding.**

| Channel | LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC | vs. 3:1 benchmark |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Referral | $291 | $10 | **28.7:1** | far above |
| Organic Search | $248 | $20 | **12.6:1** | far above |
| Content Marketing | $231 | $34 | **6.8:1** | above |
| Outbound Sales | $1,661 | $572 | **2.9:1** | just below |
| Paid Ads | $229 | $116 | **2.0:1** | below |

Paid Ads is the **second-largest spend channel** ($160,545 over 24 months,
1,388 new customers) and has the **worst unit economics** of the five.
Outbound Sales produces by far the highest LTV per customer (Enterprise-
skewed) but its CAC is so high the ratio still lands under the 3:1
benchmark. Referral is the standout — 15x cheaper CAC than Paid Ads for a
comparable-value customer — but is also the smallest channel by volume
(811 customers, $8,222 total spend).

**So what.** TaskFlow is spending the most on the two channels ($160,545 +
$158,560 = 78% of all marketing spend) that deliver the weakest returns,
while its most efficient channel is starved of budget and structurally
capped by word-of-mouth.

**Recommendation.**
1. Cap or reduce Paid Ads spend until CAC per new customer falls, or
   segment it to isolate which campaigns/audiences within Paid Ads are
   dragging the average down — don't cut blindly, since it's also the
   #2 volume channel.
2. Build a formal referral program (incentivized) to deliberately scale
   the channel that already proves the best economics, rather than
   treating it as an organic side-effect.
3. Re-evaluate Outbound Sales on a per-rep or per-deal-size basis — the
   channel's high absolute LTV suggests it may be worth keeping for
   Enterprise deals specifically even if the blended ratio undershoots
   3:1.

## Q3 — Should TaskFlow prioritize acquisition or retention next quarter?

**Finding.** Q1's answer already shows the existing base contributes a net
**−$9,186** to MRR over the full 24 months while acquisition contributes
**+$102,390**. Logo churn is improving (13.6% average in the first three
months of the dataset vs. 6.4% in the last three) but revenue churn
(3.3% average) staying well below logo churn (8.3% average) shows churn is
concentrated among lower-value (Free/Pro) accounts — higher-paying
customers are comparatively sticky already.

**So what.** Pouring more budget into acquisition — especially into
under-performing channels like Paid Ads — compounds the existing leak
instead of fixing it: every new cohort will churn at a similar rate unless
retention improves first. Since revenue churn is already lower than logo
churn, the leak is concentrated in exactly the segment (Free/entry-tier)
that's cheapest to fix with product/lifecycle work rather than expensive
re-acquisition.

**Recommendation.** Prioritize **retention** next quarter, specifically:
- Target the Free→Pro conversion and early-tenure churn window (cohort
  curves in `cohort_retention.csv` show the steepest drop in the first
  2–3 months after signup) with onboarding and activation initiatives.
- Hold acquisition spend flat, but reallocate the mix toward Referral and
  Organic Search per Q2's findings — this improves acquisition efficiency
  without needing a retention fix first.
- Revisit acquisition-led growth once NRR is trending toward 100%+; until
  then, new MRR is partially masking a retention problem rather than
  solving it.

## Limitations

- **No involuntary churn modeled** — every cancellation in this dataset is
  voluntary. Real billing data would include failed payments and expired
  cards, which need separate remediation (dunning/retry logic) from
  voluntary churn and would likely raise the logo churn figures above.
- **Gross margin (80%) is an assumption**, not a measured value — it isn't
  present in the raw data. Every LTV and LTV:CAC figure above scales
  linearly with this assumption; treat the ratios as directionally
  correct, not as exact financial figures.
- **No macro or competitive effects** — churn and growth in this dataset
  are driven only by plan, channel, and tenure. A real analysis would need
  to account for pricing changes, competitor launches, or seasonality.
- **CAC is blended per channel per month**, not per-campaign — the Paid
  Ads recommendation above (segment before cutting) reflects this: the
  data can't yet show *which* campaigns within a channel are underwater.
